------------------------- Personal Loans Credit Policy: Simplified Scorecard -------------------------

---Business context---
A consumer lender wants to evaluate an internal, data-driven approval policy for its unsecured
personal loan product. Management needs a transparent model to:
(a) Rank applicants objectively by credit risk, using only their application data.
(b) Set an approval threshold that balances commercial growth (approval volume), portfolio
    default risk and profitability.
The Lending Club accepted-loans dataset serves as the historical origination database.


---Objective---
Build an interpretable credit scoring model to predict default risk, and use it to determine the
approval cut-off that keeps portfolio defaults within an acceptable risk appetite while remaining
profitable.


---Scope and key assumptions---
Population:   36-month loans only, ensuring all included contracts have reached a terminal outcome.
Time window:  Vintages 2012-2014 for model development (training), 2015 for out-of-time
              evaluation (testing). Provisional: confirmed once data availability by vintage is checked.
              Vintages from 2016 onward are excluded due to right-censoring (outcomes not yet
              observed). Vintages 2007-2011 are excluded due to low volume and distinct economic
              conditions.
Target:       Bad (1) = Charged Off or Default; Good (0) = Fully Paid.
              Active loans ("Current") are excluded because their outcome is not yet known.
Features:     Application-time information only (income, loan amount, DTI, credit history).
              Post-origination fields (payments, recoveries, status flags) are excluded to
              prevent data leakage.
Exclusions:   Lending Club's own rating and pricing fields (grade, sub_grade, int_rate) are not
              model inputs: they already embed the lender's own risk assessment, so using them
              would make the model circular. int_rate and installment are used only to price
              loans in the profit calculation.
Economics:    Expected profit per loan = expected interest income - expected credit loss
              (PD x LGD x EAD) - funding cost. LGD is fixed at a conservative 60-70% and funding
              cost is an explicit assumption.


---Core business questions---

Question 1: Who poses the highest risk of non-payment? (Risk and Discrimination)
"Based on customer information available at the time of application (income, debt-to-income
ratio, credit history), can we predict who will default, and how well?"
   - Which variables best separate good from bad borrowers (IV), and how do they relate to risk?
   - What discriminatory power does the model achieve out of time (AUC, Gini, KS)?
   - Are the predicted probabilities well calibrated against observed default rates?
   The answer is written from the results (coefficients, IV and metrics), describing
   associations with default risk, not causes.

Question 2: Where do we set the approval threshold to avoid losing money? (Policy and Decision)
"If we set a maximum risk tolerance (a cut-off on the score), what share of applicants do we
approve, what default rate does the approved portfolio have, and which cut-off maximises
expected profit?"
   - Approval rate vs. bad rate for cut-offs defined by score percentiles, compared with the
     baseline (bad rate when approving everyone).
   - Expected profit by cut-off, and how sensitive the best cut-off is to the funding cost
     assumption.


---Notebook structure---
1. EDA: columns, observations and variable types
2. Target, population and vintages: bad rate by vintage, share of resolved loans, baseline risk
3. Variable availability: missing values by vintage
4. Variable selection: IV, correlation and VIF
5. Model: logistic regression
